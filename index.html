The Pigeonhole Principle in action

Can every exam get its own time slot?

Explore how a simple mathematical principle can reveal unavoidable scheduling overlaps when the number of examinations exceeds the number of available slots.

How the mathematics works

The generalized Pigeonhole Principle gives a guaranteed lower bound, not necessarily the exact occupancy of every slot.

1

Identify the objects and boxes

The examinations are the objects, and the available time slots are the boxes.

2

Apply the formula

Divide the number of examinations by the number of slots and round the result up: ⌈N/k⌉.

3

Interpret the result

If the answer is 2 or more, at least two examinations must share a slot in any assignment.

Important interpretation: Sharing a slot creates a real clash for a student only if that student is required to sit both examinations. The principle proves a shared slot is unavoidable; student-level conflict depends on which examinations overlap in enrolment.
